Steady Read: a short guide

Steady Read scrolls the page you are reading, slowly and steadily, so you do not have to keep swiping. You start it, and you read. This guide is for someone setting it up on their own phone, on their own or with someone helping.

First: who this is not for

If moving text makes you feel sick or dizzy, this is the wrong app for you. Steady Read moves the whole page smoothly and continuously. If that kind of motion is uncomfortable, what you want is something that jumps a whole page at a time, not something that scrolls. Please do not install this one.

What it does, and what it does not do

It scrolls the page you are reading at a steady pace, in whatever app you are already reading in, so you do not have to keep swiping.

What it does not do:

  • It does not know what is on your screen. It moves the page; it cannot see the words.
  • It does not read anything aloud. It is not a screen reader.
  • It will not notice if the page jumps on its own or an advertisement loads and shifts things. It cannot tell the page moved, so it just keeps scrolling at the pace you set.

Why it works this way, and how you can check it for yourself, is on the how to check these claims page and in the privacy policy.

Installing it

Steady Read is given out through a Google Play closed test. You open the opt-in link once, on the phone you will use it on, and accept the invitation; then you install it from Google Play like any other app.

Turning on the accessibility service

This is the one step that looks alarming, and it is worth reading slowly.

After you install Steady Read and open it, it will ask you to turn on its accessibility service. When you do, Android (not Steady Read) shows a strongly worded warning. It describes the broadest things accessibility services can do, because it is written to cover all of them.

What Steady Read actually asks Android for is one thing: permission to scroll, to dispatch a scroll gesture. Android grants it nothing more. It has no way to read the text or images on your screen, and no way to use the internet. This is not us promising to behave; Android itself enforces it, and you can confirm it yourself on the how to check these claims page.

In the app, tap Open accessibility settings, find Steady Read in the list, turn it on, and accept Android's prompt. If you are setting this up with someone helping you, this is a good moment to have them read the warning with you.

Using it

Once the service is on, you control Steady Read from the notification, the one titled Auto-scroll control in your notification drawer at the top of the screen. You do not go back into the app to use it.

  • Start and stop: open the notification drawer and tap Start to begin scrolling, and Stop to stop. After you tap Start there is a short pause before scrolling begins; that is normal. If you have just stopped it, wait a moment before tapping Start again, or the tap may not take.
  • Touch the screen to stop: while it is scrolling, touching the screen stops it immediately. This is always the fastest and most reliable way to stop, and it works no matter how it was started.
  • Proximity (on phones that have the sensor): if you turn on Proximity control in the app, you can cover the top of the phone to start or stop scrolling, without touching the screen. Bring your hand close, within about 2 cm of the top of the phone, and it may take a try to find the spot. Not every phone has the sensor this needs; if yours does not, the app tells you so and this option simply is not available.
  • Speed: in the app's Scrolling speed setting you can choose Slow, Normal, or Fast. It starts on Normal. If that feels too fast or too slow, you can change it any time.
